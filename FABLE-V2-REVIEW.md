# Fable v2: making Command Centre worth £299 a month to a clinic

Branch `fable-v2`, built from `main` at `065bf0f` (exactly what is live). Nothing has been pushed, merged or deployed, and no live service, key or database was touched: every test runs against faked networks. Both type-checks, all 83 tests (60 before, 23 new) and both builds pass. Screenshots before and after, desktop (1280) and phone (390), are in `/tmp/claude-0/-home-claude/33f7761b-12f0-5a26-b061-cf4a0d30e834/scratchpad/fable-v2/` (`before-*.png`, `after-*.png`).

## What I found, ranked by how much it hurt

1. **Every save of Your business failed with a JSON error, even though the data was saved.** The browser called `res.json()` on PostgREST's answer to the profile upsert, which is `201 Created` with an *empty* body. Chrome says "Unexpected end of JSON input", Safari "JSON Parse error: Unexpected EOF". The last step of onboarding uses the same call, so "Start my agents" showed an error too; refreshing got you in because the row had been written. This is what the owner met when he added Instagram and Facebook. Database error text could also reach the screen unchanged.
2. **Competitors were not local.** The nearby search used Google's `locationBias` (a preference, not a restriction) at 8 km, kept anything within 25 km, and only had coordinates at all when the client had already connected a Google listing. A new client, or one without a listing, got `"aesthetics clinic near Crewe"` with no coordinates, so every result had no distance and the 25 km filter kept everything. Once that first search had run, the next one was a month away, even after the listing was connected. For Crewe that produced clinics in Stoke, Chester and Warrington.
3. **Businesses without websites were half invisible.** Google's `websiteUri` for a clinic is often its Instagram page or its Fresha booking page. The code treated that as the website, tried to read it as one, and found nothing. Competitors with no website (most of the market) got no prices, no team size, no Instagram.
4. **Ask could lose the answer.** The answer was produced inside a synchronous Netlify function (10-second limit in production), returned once, and never stored anywhere the app looked again. Any hiccup between server and phone (a locked screen, a slow network, a timeout) lost it silently: the typing dots stopped and nothing appeared. The history query also returned the *oldest* 100 messages (`order=asc&limit=100`), so after a hundred messages new replies never showed on reload. His log entry (`chat`, 8.5 s, no error) fits a reply that was written and never reached the screen.
5. **No real figures on either side.** The client's own stats were only the Google rating; competitors had rating and review count. Nothing about team size, booking-page reviews, treatments listed, starting price, or how any of it compared.
6. **Prices were compared by AI guesswork.** One weekly fast-model call paired "comparable" items from page text with no notion of units, so a 0.5 ml lip price could sit against a 1 ml one, "from £150" counted as £150, and the owner's own prices only existed if their website published them.
7. **The Scout looked for companies for a business whose customers are people.** For a clinic, "businesses that could refer customers" is a side channel; the people asking *right now* for lip filler near Crewe, on public pages, were not looked at.
8. **Your business looked generated.** A 300-pixel padlock (an unsized SVG) dominated the page above seven identical definition lists. Nothing on it was about the business as the agents know it.

Smaller things found on the way: an app-wide habit of showing `e.message` to the owner (now filtered); the `competitors` function's "watch" action dropping a found business's links; "small business" nowhere (good); the `today` list unaware of prices.

## What stays the same

Sign-in, Home, the Brief, Results, Settings, the Reviews and Speed tabs, the lead pipeline, public contracts, the Stripe webhook, email, the daily schedules, Instagram through Business Discovery, the learning rules, the design language (Cormorant Garamond for what the agents say and for figures, Manrope for the interface, black and champagne). No API route, request or response shape changed except `/api/chat`, which now returns `{ queued, question, reply? }` (the app handles both shapes). Everything new in the Watcher's state lives in the existing `watcher_state.state` JSON. Old states render as before: `foundFrom`, `comparison`, `venue` and the rest are optional.

## Decisions for you

1. **Radius ladder: 5, 10, 15 km, "enough" = 5.** `RADII_KM` and `ENOUGH_NEARBY` in `shared/geo.ts`. Rural clients get 15 km; a Crewe clinic will usually stop at 5 or 10. If you would rather never pass 10 km, drop the last rung.
2. **What counts as comparable (`relevanceOf`).** A barber, a nail bar or a hair salon is not an aesthetics competitor unless its name says it does aesthetics; a spa with a vague name is watched only when nothing better is found. The owner can still "Watch" anything from the nearby list and say "Not a competitor" to anything found.
3. **Fresha area pages.** Fresha's public landing pages (`/lp/en/tt/<treatment>/in/gb-<town>/<county>`) list venues with prices per treatment and would find Fresha-only competitors Google misses. The venue-page reader is built and tested; I did not add the area-page fetch because the slug pattern (town and county) is a guess per client, and a wrong guess is a wasted fetch a day. Worth a short manual check of two or three real slugs before switching it on.
4. **Pasted price list vs AI parsing.** The list is parsed by rule (one line, a £ price), which handles everything I threw at it, and the owner sees the rows before saving. I did not add an AI pass for free text; if owners paste paragraphs rather than lines, that is the next step.
5. **The Scout's focus is encoded in the query** (`[demand] People asking…`) because `scout_searches.kind` is constrained to two values and I preferred no migration. It is invisible to the owner. A tidy `focus` column would be the proper fix; `shared/scout.ts` has the two functions to swap.
6. **Ask now costs one background function invocation per question** on top of the AI call, and answers arrive about a second later than before because of the hand-off. In return they cannot be lost. If you would rather keep it synchronous, set `INTERNAL_SECRET` empty and the inline fallback is used (not recommended).
7. **`PLACES_MONTHLY_CAP` default is 90** (was 60): the monthly search can cost up to 8 calls, plus weekly ratings for up to 9 competitors. With Google's monthly free allowance per SKU this is still free for the first dozens of clients, but keep the budget alert.
8. **Size verdict thresholds** (`sizeVerdict`): a figure counts as "more" at 1.25× (1.5× for new reviews this month). Plain numbers at the top of the function.

## Migrations and environment

- **No migration.** Everything new lives in existing JSON columns and the query text. `supabase/migrations` is untouched.
- **No new environment variables.** postcodes.io needs no key. `PLACES_MONTHLY_CAP` has a new default (90) and is documented in `.env.example`; set it explicitly if you want 60.
- `GOOGLE_API_KEY` must have **Places API (New)** enabled (it does): Nearby Search and Text Search with `locationRestriction` are both part of it.

## What's not done, ranked by value

1. **Fresha area pages for discovery** (decision 3): the most direct way to find Fresha-only competitors Google under-ranks, with their prices, in one fetch per treatment per town.
2. **Owner-connected Instagram** (reach, profile visits, messages) still needs Meta App Review; what the owner sees of their own account is the same public view competitors get.
3. **A daily "prices changed near you" email** when a competitor moves a price on a treatment the owner lists: the events exist; the email is a short addition to `daily-background`.
4. **Reading the owner's Phorest/Pabau page with a headless browser** would replace the paste step for those systems; it needs a browser in a function (Playwright on Netlify is heavy) and is better done as a separate scheduled job.
5. **Treatment catalogue breadth outside aesthetics and beauty** (physio, dental, fitness): the structure is there; the entries are not. Those businesses fall back to the AI price table, as before.
6. **A map** of the competitors around the client: distances are there; a static map image would make "nearby" visible at a glance.
7. **Tests for the Netlify handlers with a stubbed database beyond `chat` and `competitors`** (`places`, `leads`, `scout-start` with the focus marker).
